# PRODUCT ECOSYSTEM MAP

*Governed by THE_HEART_OF_THE_PROJECT, PRODUCT_MANIFESTO, EXPERIENCE_BIBLE, EXPERIENCE_RULEBOOK and PRODUCT_RECONSTRUCTION_SPECIFICATION. Those documents describe the product. This one describes the world the product lives in.*

*It contains no screens, no interfaces, and no user experience design. It describes relationships, flows, participation and amplification.*

**The question it answers:** how does someone who has never heard of Unlocking Bulgaria become someone who naturally causes another person to discover Bulgaria?

---

# PART 1 — THE ECOSYSTEM

## What the product actually is

Not an application. Not a website. Not content.

**The product is the movement of people toward extraordinary real places.**

The unit of production is not a feature or a screen. It is **a journey that happened** — one car, one Saturday, one family who now know something they did not know on Friday. Everything in this ecosystem is either causing journeys, accompanying journeys, or living off the memory of journeys.

## The three zones

**Zone 1 — The world we do not control.**
Google, TikTok, a friend at dinner, a hotel receptionist, a teacher, a road, a newspaper. **The overwhelming majority of journeys begin here.** We can seed this zone. We can never own it.

**Zone 2 — The surfaces we own.**
The website, the application, printed material, whatever we put at a site. Narrow, cheap, and easily over-invested in. Their only jobs: convert an existing curiosity into a departure, accompany the departure, and get out of the way on arrival.

**Zone 3 — The place itself.**
The ground, the light, the community that lives beside it, the road in, the coffee stand, the person who unlocks the gate. **This is where all the value is actually created, and it is where we are least present.**

> Most companies put ninety percent of their effort into Zone 2. Zone 2 is the smallest and least important of the three. Our effort belongs in Zone 1 and Zone 3.

## Where the application fits

The application is the **last mile and the arrival**. It is the only surface that can know you have arrived and go quiet at the right second. That is its irreplaceable function. Everything else it currently does can be done better by the website, by paper, or by a human being.

## Where the website fits

The website is **the case for going** — the argument, the cost, the proof, and the thing you send to one other person. It is the ecosystem's public memory and its search surface. It carries almost all of the acquisition load and always will.

## Where reality begins

Reality begins the moment someone says a sentence out loud to another person. Everything before that is preparation. Everything after it is consequence.

## The real asset

Not the app. Not the brand. Not the content library.

**The retellable sentence.**

One place, one sentence. It is the only artifact that travels through every surface in the ecosystem without modification — a search snippet, a video caption, a receptionist's mouth, a classroom, a message to one friend, a line on a printed card, a plaque on a wall. It requires no install, works with no signal, survives translation, and costs nothing to distribute.

**The ecosystem is, structurally, a distribution network for sentences that cause driving.**

# PART 2 — ENTRY POINTS

Every entry point falls into one of four intents. The intent — not the channel — determines what should happen next.

## Intent A — AMBIENT (not looking for anything)

*Emotional state: idle. Question being asked: none. Our job: cause a snag.*

| Entry point | What should happen next |
|---|---|
| **Instagram / TikTok / Reels** | One fact, one location, one distance. Never a beautiful landscape alone — beauty produces admiration and admiration is satisfied at a distance. Show the ordinary thing and say the extraordinary fact. |
| **YouTube** | Long-form belongs to a *person*, not the brand — someone walking somewhere and being surprised. |
| **Facebook groups (Bulgarian, diaspora, hiking, 4×4, parents)** | Peer-to-peer only. A brand post in a hiking group is noise; a member's photo with a fact is the whole product. |
| **A friend at dinner** | The single highest-converting entry point in the ecosystem. Nothing to build. Everything depends on them having a sentence. |
| **Newspaper / podcast / TV** | Never "an app was launched." Always "here is a thing about this country you did not know." The product is a footnote to the fact. |
| **A road sign** | Someone at 90 km/h who suddenly knows a name. Costs nothing per impression and lasts twenty years. |

## Intent B — PLANNING (has time, no plan)

*Emotional state: mild pressure. Question: what should we do tomorrow? Our job: propose one place with an honest cost.*

| Entry point | What should happen next |
|---|---|
| **Google — "what to do near Lovech", "with kids", "day trip from Sofia"** | The largest addressable intent in the ecosystem. Answer the question they typed, then give one place with distance, duration, difficulty and child viability. |
| **AI assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews)** | A growing share of "where should we go" now resolves inside a model. Our content must be structured, factual, quotable and attributable, or we are invisible in the fastest-growing planning surface in the world. |
| **Hotel or guesthouse reception** | *"You have tomorrow free? Go here."* A human recommendation outperforms every digital surface we will ever build. |
| **Travel blog / forum** | Long-tail, high-trust, low-volume. Feed it facts, not press releases. |
| **Tourist information centre** | People who have already decided to explore and only need a target. |

## Intent C — SITUATED (physically there, right now)

*Emotional state: mild curiosity, some confusion. Question: what am I actually looking at? Our job: give the astonishment on the spot.*

| Entry point | What should happen next |
|---|---|
| **QR at the site** | **Must deliver a sentence, not an install.** A QR at a cave that opens an app store is a wasted encounter and a small insult. |
| **Museum / archaeological site / cave / church / national park** | The visitor is already in the emotional posture we spend everything to create. Meet them there. |
| **Restaurant, coffee stand, campsite near a site** | The three-kilometre radius around every place is unclaimed territory. |
| **Festival, village fair, local event** | People already outdoors, already open, already local. |

## Intent D — INSTITUTIONAL (professional interest)

*Emotional state: evaluative. Question: can I use this? Our job: hand them something they can hand on.*

| Entry point | What should happen next |
|---|---|
| **Teacher** | The highest multiplier in the ecosystem. One teacher reaches twenty-five households in an afternoon. |
| **Municipality** | Wants visitors, dwell time and a reason to fund access. |
| **Museum / heritage professional** | Wants accuracy and attribution, and will destroy us publicly if we invent. |
| **Guide / tour operator** | Wants material that makes them look knowledgeable. |
| **Conference, exhibition, tourism fair** | Institutional legitimacy; slow, high-value. |

## The rule that governs all entry points

> **No entry point may require an install to deliver its first value.**

Every encounter must be complete in itself. Someone who reads a sentence on a sign, is astonished, and drives there has received the entire product and given us nothing. **That is a success, not a leak.**

# PART 3 — THE JOURNEYS

*All thirteen begin outside anything we own.*

**Family with two children.** Thursday, a parent sees a fifteen-second video of a cave ceiling with two holes in it. Saturday they drive. The eight-year-old finds the path first. On Monday the child tells their class, and two of those households drive there within a month. *The child is the distribution mechanism.*

**Young couple.** Looking for something that isn't a restaurant. Enter through Instagram or a friend. They want a day that photographs well and feels like a discovery nobody else made. They will post — which is the payment.

**Solo traveller.** Google, at 23:00, from a hotel room in Sofia. Needs: reachable without a car, honest difficulty, and no requirement to be sociable.

**Foreign tourist.** Arrives with three days and a rented car. Entry is a guesthouse owner or a search. **Language is the constraint: English, present and correct, or they never enter.**

**Teacher.** Enters through a colleague or a municipal programme. Needs curriculum fit, a bus-viable location, toilets, and a way to make twenty-five children pay attention for forty minutes.

**School class.** Does not choose. Arrives. Some are bored. Three are not, and those three go home and make their parents drive. *We are not designing for the class. We are designing for the three.*

**Hotel guest.** Asks at reception. Zero digital contact until after the recommendation. **This journey can be completed with no app and no website.**

**Weekend traveller (Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna).** The core repeat audience. Radius of two hours, appetite for six to twelve outings a year, and the ecosystem's most valuable long-term relationship.

**Photographer.** Enters through the light, not the story. Wants season, hour, direction, and permission. Produces the images that seed Zone 1 — an unpaid content engine if treated with respect.

**Cyclist / motorcyclist.** Route-shaped thinking. Wants places strung along a road, surface conditions, water, and shade.

**Camper / van traveller.** Wants proximity, legality of overnighting, and dark skies. Underserved by everything in Bulgaria today.

**Retired couple.** Weekday travellers, off-season, unhurried — the ecosystem's most valuable customers for local businesses because they arrive when nobody else does. Requirements: readable text, short walks, honest difficulty, toilets, somewhere to have lunch.

**Local resident.** Lives twenty minutes away and has never been. Enters through a Facebook group or a neighbour. The most emotionally powerful journey in the ecosystem: *I did not know this was in my own municipality.* They become the ecosystem's most credible advocates.

# PART 5 — THE FLOW OF CURIOSITY

*Twelve transitions. For each, what causes it — and what kills it. The failure modes matter more than the mechanisms.*

**Ignorance → Discovery.** *Caused by:* one fact, out of scale with its setting, arriving unbidden. *Killed by:* a beautiful photograph. Beauty produces admiration; admiration is satisfied at a distance and the person never goes.

**Discovery → Interest.** *Caused by:* proximity. The fact becomes personal the second it has a distance attached. *Killed by:* not saying where it is.

**Interest → Decision.** *Caused by:* the cost being answered before it is asked — how long, how hard, what time, can the children manage. *Killed by:* silence about cost. **This is the largest leak in the entire ecosystem and it is a content problem, not a product problem.**

**Decision → Planning.** *Caused by:* saying it out loud to someone else. *Killed by:* having nobody to say it to, or the other person having no reason to care. Journeys are social; a decision held privately usually dies.

**Planning → Travel.** *Caused by:* a date and a departure time. *Killed by:* weather, and by nothing offering an alternative when the weather turns. **The ecosystem currently has no answer for a rained-out Saturday.**

**Travel → Arrival.** *Caused by:* the road being right. *Killed by:* doubt at the threshold — the last kilometres, no signs, narrowing road. More cars turn around here than anyone measures.

**Arrival → Wonder.** *Caused by:* silence, and being in the right spot facing the right way. *Killed by:* anything speaking.

**Wonder → Memory.** *Caused by:* time and quiet immediately afterwards. *Killed by:* proposing the next thing while the memory is still setting.

**Memory → Story.** *Caused by:* having a sentence worth repeating. *Killed by:* remembering that it was good but not being able to say why. **A person who cannot retell it is a dead end in the ecosystem, however happy they are.**

**Story → Recommendation.** *Caused by:* the listener asking *where is that?* *Killed by:* the teller not knowing the cost — "somewhere near Lovech, I think" converts nobody.

**Recommendation → New person.** *Caused by:* the listener being able to find it in ten seconds. *Killed by:* us being unfindable under the words a normal person would actually use.

**New person → Ignorance closed.** The loop closes **outside every surface we own**, in a conversation we will never see and cannot measure. Design for it anyway. It is the majority of the growth.

# PART 6 — THE PRODUCT LOOPS

| Loop | Starts with | Kept alive by | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | One sentence entering Zone 1 | New astonishments authored at a steady rate | The astonishments run out. **Content authoring rate is the governor on the entire ecosystem.** |
| **Family** | A parent seeing a fact | The child becoming the one who asks to go again | Places stop being child-viable, or the child outgrows it |
| **Weekend** | A free Saturday within two hours of a city | Rotation, novelty, honest difficulty | **Everything within radius is done — a real ceiling.** Answered by season, not by more places |
| **Education** | One teacher | Curriculum fit and a trip that is easy to organise | The teacher moves school, or the bus becomes unaffordable |
| **Municipality** | Visitors appearing without being asked for | Local revenue justifying signage, access and maintenance | Over-development. **Success is the failure mode here** |
| **Business** | A guesthouse or coffee stand noticing new customers | Being given sentences worth repeating and getting paid in footfall | The business changes hands and the knowledge leaves with the owner |
| **Content** | A journey producing photographs and stories | Visitors becoming sources — the cheapest content engine available | Nobody asks the visitor what they found |
| **Recommendation** | Someone saying a sentence | Every visitor leaving with one worth saying | The retellable sentence was never written. **This loop is the master loop; all others feed it** |
| **Seasonal** | The same place in snow, at dawn, in the month it means something | Deliberate second-visit propositions | We treat places as consumed after one visit |

**The two loops that matter most and cost least:** Education and Recommendation. One teacher reaches twenty-five households in an afternoon. One good sentence reaches everyone the visitor talks to for a year. Neither requires engineering.

# PART 7 — PARTNERS

## The principle

**Partners are not a distribution channel. They are the last human in the chain.**

A guesthouse owner saying *"tomorrow, go here — it is forty minutes and the children will manage it"* outperforms every digital surface in this ecosystem. Therefore the partner product is not a portal, a dashboard, or a revenue-share agreement.

> **The partner product is a card with three sentences on it and a reason to say them.**

Three places within thirty minutes, one astonishment each, honest costs, printed. That is the entire v1 partner programme. No login, no integration, no accounting.

| Partner | Value they create | Value they receive |
|---|---|---|
| **Hotels & guesthouses** | The recommendation at the exact moment of need | Guests who stay an extra night; a host who looks knowledgeable |
| **Restaurants & coffee stands** | Receive the visitor well; make the day work | Predictable footfall in the shoulder hours |
| **Municipalities** | Access, parking, signage, safety, legitimacy | Visitors in villages that receive none; a reason to fund maintenance |
| **Museums & heritage institutions** | Accuracy, authority, permission | Audiences arriving already interested and already informed |
| **Archaeological sites, caves, parks** | The place itself, and the rules for treating it properly | Visitors who understand what they are standing in and behave accordingly |
| **Churches & monasteries** | Living context, and the boundary of what may be told | Respectful visitors; recorded and correct traditions |
| **Schools** | Multiplication — twenty-five households per afternoon | Trips that are easy to justify and organise |
| **Guides & tour operators** | Depth we cannot deliver; the human presence | Material that makes them the expert |
| **Tourist information centres** | The target for people already committed | Something specific to say |
| **Festivals & local events** | Concentrated attention, already outdoors | Attendance from outside the village |
| **Local historians & elders** | **The best material in the ecosystem, and the consent that makes it usable** | Their version recorded, attributed, and outliving them |

**The non-negotiable:** where a place is inhabited, sacred, or actively used, the custodial community's version outranks ours, and their non-objection precedes publication. This is both an ethical requirement and the ecosystem's cheapest insurance policy — a community that feels robbed can close a site permanently.

# PART 8 — CONTENT ECOSYSTEM

**Where it originates.** Three sources, in ascending order of value:
1. **Scholarship** — published, citable, safe, and available to anyone. Necessary, not differentiating.
2. **The field** — what is actually there now: the road surface, the fence, the hours, the light at 09:00 in March. Nobody else has this and it decays constantly.
3. **The community** — the local historian, the priest, the woman who has lived beside it for sixty years. **The best material and the least used.** Gathering it is simultaneously the research method and the consent mechanism.

**Who creates.** A small authoring group — one or two people who understand disproportion — working from all three sources. This is the ecosystem's bottleneck and should be staffed accordingly.

**Who curates.** One editor with the authority to reject. The most important job in the ecosystem is saying *this place is not astonishing enough.*

**Who verifies.** A historian for claims; the field for conditions; the community for meaning. Nothing is invented, ever — the reputational downside is total and permanent.

**Who translates.** Bulgarian is the master; everything else carries the meaning forward, never the English phrasing. **Realistically: Bulgarian and English. Twelve further languages are a success problem, and paying for them before success is theatre.**

**Who updates.** Nobody has planned for this, and **content decays faster than anyone expects** — hours change, a road closes, a site gets fenced, an owner dies. Every place needs a re-verification cadence, and the cheapest verifier in the world is the visitor who was there last Saturday. **Ask them. Nothing currently does.**

**How it spreads.** Sentence → search snippet → video caption → receptionist → classroom → message to one friend → printed card → sign at the site. One artifact, every surface, no reformatting.

# PART 9 — THE LIFETIME JOURNEY

**This weekend.** One place. It is real, it is close, and it was better than expected. They tell four people. Relationship: *a good tip.*

**This month.** A second place, different in character. The suspicion forms: *someone chose these.* They start noticing landscape from the car differently. Relationship: *a source.*

**This year.** Six to ten places. They have taken someone else at least once. They have recommended unprompted, probably in a Facebook group, probably without mentioning us. They now have opinions about which places are best. Relationship: *a habit.*

**Five years.** Forty to sixty places, across seasons. They have returned to places they thought were finished and found them different in snow. They correct strangers online. They are mildly protective. Their children — if they have them — have grown up doing this and treat it as normal. Relationship: *identity.*

**Ten years.** They read landscape fluently: a smooth rise in a field is not a hill. They are more interested in bringing people than in going somewhere new. They may be contributing — telling us about a place we do not have, or correcting something we got wrong. Their child may now be the one driving.

**Nobody will ever tell them what they have become, and they will not have a word for it.** That is correct. The role, not more content, is what the ecosystem owes them at this point.

# PART 10 — THE FLYWHEEL

```
More places authored to the astonishment standard
      ↓  (each yields one retellable sentence)
More journeys taken
      ↓  (people cannot help retelling)
More stories in Zone 1 — dinner tables, groups, videos, classrooms
      ↓  (stories create name-searches: "prohodna", "магура", "belogradchik")
More search demand for specific places
      ↓  (demand we own, because we hold the best content for those names)
More visitors arriving in villages that receive none
      ↓  (footfall becomes revenue)
Stronger local businesses and more motivated municipalities
      ↓  (revenue funds access, signage, maintenance, permission)
Better access and deeper community relationships
      ↓  (elders, historians, custodians open up)
Better content — the material nobody else can get
      ↓
More places authored to the astonishment standard
```

**Every connection, and where each can break:**

*Places → journeys* — breaks if the cost is not answered honestly.
*Journeys → stories* — breaks if the visitor has no sentence. **The single most preventable break in the system.**
*Stories → searches* — breaks if we are unfindable under ordinary words.
*Searches → visitors* — breaks if the answer arrives without a distance.
*Visitors → local revenue* — breaks if there is nowhere to buy a coffee, which is a village-level fact we can influence but not control.
*Revenue → municipal motivation* — breaks if the value is invisible to the municipality. **Make it visible: tell them the numbers.**
*Access → community relationships* — breaks if a community feels extracted from.
*Community → better content* — breaks if consent was never sought.

**The governor.** The flywheel is powered by astonishments, and astonishments are finite, slow, and hand-made. Everything downstream is constrained by how many places can be authored to standard per month. **Every roadmap in this project is fiction until that rate is measured.**

**The counter-flywheel.** Success has its own spiral and it is the standard failure mode of cultural tourism:

> More visitors → site degradation and local irritation → access restricted or the place ruined → fewer places worth going to.

Defences: refuse to publish places that cannot receive visitors; never optimise for volume; make the community a beneficiary before they become a victim; and treat *choosing not to send anyone* as a legitimate outcome of the work.

# PART 12 — THE MISSING PIECES

*What is absent from the ecosystem — not from the app.*

**1. Any physical presence at all.** The entire ecosystem is currently digital. The most powerful recommendation in the world is one person handing another person a thing. There is nothing to hand.

**2. Presence at the place itself.** A person standing at the cave right now — the exact emotional state we spend everything to create — has no way in. No sign, no plaque, no QR, no card at the entrance. **This is the cheapest unexploited opportunity in the ecosystem.**

**3. A single signed municipal partner.** Partnerships are described in the abstract across several documents. Zero are signed. One real relationship with one municipality would teach more than all of it.

**4. Anyone owning the three-kilometre radius.** The coffee stand, the guesthouse, the person who unlocks the gate — the businesses that benefit most do not know we exist.

**5. A teacher product.** The highest-multiplier channel has nothing built for it: no lesson, no bus-viable list, no permission slip, no forty-minute structure.

**6. Google Maps presence.** Free, enormous, and ignored. Every place should exist there with correct hours, access notes and photographs.

**7. A content maintenance model.** Everything assumes content is authored once and stays true. It will not.

**8. A way for visitors to contribute.** The richest source of new astonishments is the person who just went somewhere and found something. Nothing collects it.

**9. A named human.** The stated experience is *a friend who knows this country impossibly well.* **The ecosystem currently has no friend** — no author, no guide, no historian anyone can follow, argue with, or trust. Ecosystems spread through people. This one has a logo where a person should be.

**10. An answer for bad weather.** A rained-out Saturday currently ends the journey rather than redirecting it.

**11. An answer for "everything within two hours is done."** The weekend loop has a real ceiling. Seasons are the answer and nothing is built around them.

**12. Anything offline in the villages.** Part of the ecosystem's most important audience — the people who live beside these places — is not reachable digitally at all.

# THE FINAL TEST

> **If the mobile application disappeared tomorrow, would the ecosystem still create curiosity, travel and memories?**

**Honest answer: mostly yes — and that is an indictment, not a compliment.**

Curiosity survives entirely. It lives in Zone 1, where the app has never been. Travel survives — the website already carries the case for going better than the app does, and navigation was always going to be handed off. Memory survives, because memory was always made of wind, light and who was with you.

**What dies is the arrival.** Nothing else can know you have crossed a threshold and go quiet at the right second. That is the app's one irreplaceable function, and it happens to be the moment where the transformation actually occurs.

So the ecosystem survives the app's disappearance, and the *transformation degrades*.

**Three things would make the answer a full yes**, and none of them are software:

- **A sentence at the site** — a sign, a plaque, a card, a QR that answers without installing anything.
- **A human who tells you** — a receptionist, a teacher, a guide, a neighbour, holding three sentences.
- **A printed route** — that works with no battery, no signal, and no interest in software.

Build those three and the ecosystem becomes genuinely independent of the application — at which point the application stops being the product and becomes what it should always have been:

**the most powerful interface inside a much larger living thing.**
