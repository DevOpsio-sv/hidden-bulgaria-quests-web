# EXPERIENCE HYPOTHESES
## Field manual for validating the Prohodna slice

*Purpose: stop internal conviction being mistaken for external truth. Everything in the governing documents is well-argued and none of it has been observed. Each item below is a claim to test, not a belief to defend. Open this during planning meetings.*

**Honest headline:** almost nothing in this product is Proven. Most is Strong or Weak hypothesis; the two most important claims are **Unknown**. The single most important line in this manual: the core mechanism — protected silence at arrival — is Unknown and has **no fallback in any document**. Validate it first, cheaply, before finalising arrival code.

---

## HOW TO READ

**Confidence** = our belief in the hypothesis before testing (Very Low / Low / Medium / High). Not confidence in evidence.

**Classification** = evidence state: Proven · Supported · Strong · Weak · Unknown.

**Validation Stage** = when it can be tested: Remote interview · Prototype test · Lab usability · Field observation · Live pilot · Long-term.

**Tiers:** Tier 1 = product killers (failure changes direction). Tier 2 = shapers (failure changes implementation). Tier 3 = optimisers (tune over time). **Pre-conditions** = pass/fail gates before any family is invited.

---

## COMMON VALIDATION METHODS

Referenced below by code. Defined once here.

**M1 · Anchor Field Session.** 15–20 groups at Prohodna, shadowed end to end, faces and hands observed, plus 1-week and 1-month recall calls. **This single session validates most of Tier 1.** Standing rules: the instrument is a person with a notebook, not a dashboard; the place is extraordinary regardless of the app, so **measure behaviour and recall, not enjoyment** (satisfaction will be uniformly high and useless); consent, right to withdraw, and local-first data are mandatory; no continuous location or attention tracking.

**M2 · Delayed Recall Call.** 1-week (and where noted 1-month) unaided phone call: "tell me about the place you went." Score what surfaces without prompting. The real dependent variable across the whole programme.

**M3 · On-site A/B.** Matched groups run different arms of the same surface within the anchor session or a follow-up visit.

**M4 · Think-aloud (remote or lab).** Planning or comprehension task, no travel required.

**M5 · Web A/B.** Website proposal / place-page variants, measured by opens and a follow-up "did you go?" call.

**M6 · Pre-condition check.** Crew-only pass/fail at the site. No participants beyond a small age-spanning group. Not an experiment — a gate.

# TIER 1 — PRODUCT KILLERS
*If any of these fails and has no acceptable remedy, the product direction changes.*

### H-SIL-1 · Silence is not read as malfunction
A deliberately blank, touch-responsive screen is understood or tolerated as intentional, not broken.
- **Why it matters** The central mechanism. If people think it froze, they tap, restart, or abandon — destroying the exact moment the product exists to create. No fallback exists anywhere.
- **How we test it** M1 — observe the silent window: pocket, tap, confuse, restart? Then interview.
- **Supports** Reflection aids encoding; the ideal state is a phone in a pocket, so nobody is watching the screen.
- **Disproves** Repeated tapping, restarts, or "I thought it froze."
- **Classification** Unknown · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** #1.

### H-JRN-1 · The whole journey holds together for a real family
A family with two children completes discovery → memory with no confusion, abandonment, or broken moment.
- **Why it matters** Every part is reasoned individually; the integrated journey has never run once. Integration is where designs die.
- **How we test it** M1 anchor study, 5–8 families, full shadow + M2.
- **Supports** Each component is independently defensible.
- **Disproves** Any consistent break point; the moment fails to land.
- **Classification** Unknown · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Field observation → Live pilot · **Priority** #2.

### H-ATT-2 · People put the phone away during the first look
During the protected window, people physically pocket or lower the phone.
- **Why it matters** If they instead stare at a blank screen, the silence backfires into anxiety. Determines whether H-SIL-1's remedy is even needed.
- **How we test it** M1 — code phone position during the window (same observation as H-SIL-1).
- **Supports** Natural behaviour at a dramatic place is to look up.
- **Disproves** Most keep the phone raised, waiting.
- **Classification** Unknown · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** #3.

### H-STO-1 · The "what is this?" question forms unprompted
After the silence, people spontaneously want the story, so it can be offered rather than pushed.
- **Why it matters** If the question doesn't form, an offer-only story goes unopened and the content is never experienced.
- **How we test it** M1 — unprompted story-open rate with zero encouragement. Target ≥60%.
- **Supports** Curiosity after an unmediated encounter is the Bible's core claim.
- **Disproves** Most never open it, or ask "what do we do now?"
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** High.

### H-GS-1 · AR as afterword is not worse than AR as the peak
Moving Guardian Sight to an optional after-the-fact layer does not lower satisfaction, and may raise realness.
- **Why it matters** Contradicts the original AR-as-climax design and the marketing built on it. Expensive to reverse. Turns Owner Decision 5 from argument into data.
- **How we test it** M3 A/B (afterword vs. moment-AR) + M2 — a week later, do they describe the *real place* or the AR?
- **Supports** The unmediated ceiling needs no technology; AR-2 forbids AR being the moment.
- **Disproves** Moment-AR group markedly more satisfied; afterword group asks why AR came so late.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Prototype test → Field A/B · **Priority** High.

### H-PLN-1 · Honest cost upfront converts intention to departure
Answering distance, duration, difficulty, hours, season and child viability beside the astonishment is what turns "interesting" into "we're going Saturday."
- **Why it matters** Cost-silence is claimed to be the largest leak in the system. If it isn't, the biggest lever is mis-identified.
- **How we test it** M5 web A/B (cost block vs. none) + "did you actually go?" call; or M4 planning simulation.
- **Supports** Decision-friction research; feasibility unknowns stall plans.
- **Disproves** No difference in journeys actually taken.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Remote / Web A/B · **Priority** High.

### H-SHR · The word-of-mouth engine works *(merges retellable sentence + one-to-one sharing)*
People can repeat the place's one sentence a week later unaided **(H-SHR-2)**, and a message to one person drives more real journeys than public broadcast would **(H-SHR-1)**.
- **Why it matters** The entire growth model is one-to-one retelling. If sentences don't stick or don't convert recipients, the ecosystem stalls — and refusing social features would be capping reach for nothing.
- **How we test it** M2 recall call: does the astonishment come back (target ≥70%)? Plus new-visitor attribution ("who told you?") to trace recipient→journey.
- **Supports** A2 is the ecosystem's core unit; personal recommendation outperforms broadcast on trust.
- **Disproves** People can't articulate what made it special; or one-to-one shares produce negligible downstream visits.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Long-term measurement · **Priority** High.

# PRE-CONDITIONS
*Gates, not experiments. Must pass before any family is invited. Crew-only (M6), plus a small age-spanning group for accessibility.*

### H-OFF-1 · Offline works at the cave
Full on-site journey runs in airplane mode at Prohodna, content cached at preparation.
- **Why it matters** If offline fails, the product fails exactly where it must work. Prohodna is a limestone gorge — assume bad signal.
- **How we test it** M6 — complete the journey, 3 devices, airplane mode, on site.
- **Classification** Strong (unverified) · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Gate.

### H-ACC-1 · The journey completes for a 70-year-old and a child, no camera
Full experience completable by older adults and children with AR disabled throughout.
- **Why it matters** The "national memory vs. private club" line. Non-negotiable inclusion.
- **How we test it** M6 — shadow one older adult and one 8–10-year-old end to end, camera off.
- **Classification** Strong (unverified) · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Gate.

### H-TYP-1 · Type is legible at arm's length in direct sun
Hierarchy levels 1–3 readable outdoors, standing, one-handed, bright and low sun.
- **How we test it** M6 — on-site read-aloud, multiple devices, ages spanning to 70+.
- **Classification** Supported (unverified) · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Gate (cheap, blocking).

# TIER 2 — PRODUCT SHAPERS
*Failure changes implementation, not philosophy.*

### H-ARR-2 · One sentence is enough to orient
A single "you are here, walk that way" gets people onto the path unaided.
- **Why it matters** If not, people feel abandoned at the one moment we promised help. Fix is a second visible referent, not a redesign.
- **How we test it** M1 — path found within 60s, no further interaction. Target ≥80%.
- **Supports** Wayfinding minimalism. **Disproves** >20% wander or ask aloud.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** High.

### H-SIL-2 · 30-second first-look window is right
30s recalls better than 20s without the tedium of 45s.
- **How we test it** M3 three arms (20/30/45s) + M2 recall of the first sight. If no difference, default to shortest.
- **Supports** Reflection improves encoding. **Disproves** No recall difference across arms.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Field A/B · **Priority** Medium.

### H-STO-2 · One idea per stop beats one rich narration
3–5 single-idea stops give better recall than one dense telling.
- **How we test it** M3 segmented vs. single + M2 distinct-fact recall and preference.
- **Supports** Chunking research. **Disproves** Single narration recalls equally and is preferred.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field A/B · **Priority** Medium.

### H-MEM-1 · The 48-hour delayed memory is opened and valued
Withholding the memory object 48h makes it more valued than immediate — and it still gets opened.
- **Why it matters** Delay's real risk is being forgotten entirely. "Never opened" is the failure mode.
- **How we test it** M3 immediate vs. +48h; measure open rate and one-to-one shares + interview on value.
- **Supports** Anticipation and consolidation. **Disproves** Delayed memory frequently never opened.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Live pilot · **Priority** High.

### H-NAV-1 · People accept the maps-app hand-off
Handing navigation to the device's maps app reads as seamless, not incomplete.
- **Why it matters** Building our own navigation is a product in itself — very costly to reverse into.
- **How we test it** M1 — observe hand-off and return, interview on seamlessness. Target ≥80% clean.
- **Supports** People already trust their maps app. **Disproves** Confusion returning, or requests for in-app directions.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** High.

### H-ATT-1 · On-site screen time stays under 25% of the visit
- **Why it matters** High screen time means the app is replacing the place — the core failure.
- **How we test it** M1 — aggregate screen-active ÷ dwell across visits. No content-level tracking.
- **Supports** The design offers little to look at. **Disproves** Screen-active exceeds 25%, especially in story.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** High.

### H-CON-1 · One astonishment beats three facts
A single disproportionate fact drives more opens and better recall than three supporting facts.
- **How we test it** M5 web A/B (one vs. three) + 1-day place recall.
- **Supports** Salience research favours single strong signals. **Disproves** Three facts convert better without recall loss.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Web A/B · **Priority** Medium.

### H-ARR-3 · No celebration is not felt as coldness
Withholding reward/sound at arrival reads as calm, not as the app doing nothing.
- **How we test it** M3 + interview: "did the app feel like it was working when you arrived?"
- **Supports** Arrival is a threshold, not an achievement. **Disproves** Multiple people report the arrival felt broken or cold.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field A/B · **Priority** Medium.

# TIER 3 — PRODUCT OPTIMISERS
*Validate over time. Not blockers for first release.*

### H-NAV-2 · Threshold doubt is real and one reassurance resolves it
- **Test** Ride-along on ~10 approaches; note doubt expressions and turn-arounds.
- **Supports** Plausible, unmeasured. **Disproves** No observable doubt with modern maps.
- **Classification** Strong/Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Medium.

### H-STO-3 · The unanswered question drives return and retelling
- **Test** M2 at 1 week and 1 month — is it raised unprompted? Compare to a resolved ending.
- **Supports** Open loops aid memory (contested). **Disproves** No difference, or reports of frustration.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Long-term · **Priority** Low.

### H-SIL-3 · No on-site music increases long-term sensory recall
- **Test** M3 music vs. none + M2 at 1 month probing what they *heard/felt*.
- **Supports** Music masks ambient sound, the durable memory. **Disproves** Music group recalls equal detail, stronger emotion.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Long-term · **Priority** Low (safe either way).

### H-GS-2 · A high AR-decline rate is acceptable
- **Test** Telemetry decline rate correlated with satisfaction and recall.
- **Supports** If AR is truly optional, decline is fine. **Disproves** Decliners report missing the point.
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Live pilot · **Priority** Medium (decides AR funding).

### H-PLN-2 · Parents weight child suitability above travel time
- **Test** M4 think-aloud trade-off pairs; which field is referenced first.
- **Supports** Intuition. **Disproves** Travel time or toilets/food dominate. Informs copy weighting only.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Low · **Stage** Remote interview · **Priority** Low.

### H-MEM-2 · Not capturing the moment is not felt as loss
- **Test** Interview: did the absence of a capture prompt bother you?
- **Supports** People photograph anyway with their own camera. **Disproves** People wanted and missed a capture affordance.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Low.

### H-MOT-1 · Static silent screens read as calm, not frozen
- **Test** Rolls into H-SIL-1 observation — note "is it frozen?" reactions.
- **Supports** Principled forbidden-motion zones. **Disproves** People behave as if crashed.
- **Classification** Weak · **Confidence** Medium · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Medium (bundled with #1).

### H-ARR-1 · Awkwardness precedes awe
- **Test** M1 — timestamp practical-settled vs. first-awe.
- **Supports** Task-switching load is documented. **Disproves** Wonder routinely precedes practical settling.
- **Classification** Supported (30s boundary Weak) · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Field observation · **Priority** Medium.

### H-TRU-1 · One extraordinary place earns the second recommendation *(deferred — needs ≥2 places)*
- **Test** Longitudinal: is second-place uptake higher among first-place completers?
- **Classification** Strong · **Confidence** High · **Stage** Long-term · **Priority** Deferred (post-slice).

# SESSION GROUPING
*Minimise field visits and participant sessions. Almost everything runs inside one anchor study.*

| Session | Validates | Participants |
|---|---|---|
| **S0 · Pre-condition day** (M6, crew + small age-spanning group) | H-OFF-1, H-ACC-1, H-TYP-1 | Crew + 1 older adult + 1 child. **Before any family.** |
| **S1 · Anchor field study** (M1, 15–20 groups, full shadow) | H-SIL-1, H-ATT-2, H-MOT-1, H-JRN-1, H-STO-1, H-ARR-1, H-ARR-2, H-NAV-1, H-ATT-1, H-ARR-3 | 15–20 groups incl. families |
| **S1-callbacks** (M2, phone) | H-SHR (recall + attribution), H-STO-3, H-JRN-1 recall | Same participants, 1wk + 1mo |
| **S2 · A/B follow-up** (M3, subset of groups) | H-SIL-2, H-STO-2, H-GS-1, H-ARR-3 arms | Matched groups |
| **Remote / Web** (M4, M5) | H-PLN-1, H-CON-1, H-PLN-2 | No travel |
| **Pilot telemetry** (live) | H-MEM-1, H-GS-2, H-SIL-3, H-TRU-1 | Live users, longitudinal |

**Key efficiency:** ten hypotheses — including the three Unknowns — resolve in the single anchor study S1. The A/B arms (S2) can piggyback on the same trip if enough groups are recruited.

# FIRST VALIDATION ROADMAP

**Week 1 — Gate the site. No families yet.**
Run S0. Confirm offline, camera-free completion, and sun legibility (H-OFF-1, H-ACC-1, H-TYP-1). Any fail here blocks everything downstream — fix before inviting anyone. Requires only a rough working build, not the polished slice. In parallel, launch the remote/web tests (H-PLN-1, H-CON-1) which need no site and no slice.

**Week 2 — Test the core mechanism cheaply.**
Before finalising arrival code, run a minimal S1 focused on the three Unknowns: H-SIL-1, H-ATT-2, H-MOT-1. This can use a rough prototype of the arrival→silence→first-look sequence, not the full journey. **If protected silence reads as broken and no remedy holds, stop and rethink the thesis — do not proceed to build.**

**Week 3 — Full anchor study.**
Complete S1 across 15–20 groups end to end (H-JRN-1, H-STO-1, H-ARR-1/2/3, H-NAV-1, H-ATT-1). Requires a working vertical slice. Recruit enough groups to fold in S2 A/B arms (H-SIL-2, H-STO-2, H-GS-1) on the same trip.

**Week 4 — Callbacks and decide.**
Run 1-week M2 calls (H-SHR recall, H-JRN-1 durability). Consolidate findings, update every classification in this manual, and make the go / iterate / stop call. Long-term items (H-MEM-1, H-GS-2, H-SIL-3, H-TRU-1) move to pilot telemetry and are reviewed after launch.
