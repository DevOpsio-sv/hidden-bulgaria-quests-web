# PRODUCT PATTERN LIBRARY

**The design language of Unlocking Bulgaria.**

*Derived from — and subordinate to — THE_HEART_OF_THE_PROJECT_v2, PRODUCT_MANIFESTO, EXPERIENCE_BIBLE, EXPERIENCE_RULEBOOK, PRODUCT_RECONSTRUCTION_SPECIFICATION and PRODUCT_ECOSYSTEM_MAP. It invents nothing. It names what those documents already do repeatedly.*

**A pattern is not a feature, a screen, a component or a technology. A pattern is a repeatable human behaviour joined to a repeatable product response.**

This library exists so that features are **composed** rather than invented. It is also intended to be the one document a new designer must read: it absorbs the operational content of the others, and consulting it should replace consulting them daily.

**Twenty-six patterns.** Distilled from roughly seventy candidates. The reduction is in Pass 2 and is the most useful page here.

# THE PATTERNS

## GROUP A — ROOT PATTERNS
*Two patterns. Everything else in the library serves one of them.*

---

### A1 · THE DISPROPORTION

**Purpose** Make a place worth crossing distance for.
**Human situation** A person is surrounded by unremarkable-looking things and has no reason to stop at any of them.
**Trigger** Authoring a place, before publication.
**Desired emotional transition** Indifference → involuntary attention.
**Problem solved** Beauty produces admiration, and admiration is satisfied at a distance. Nobody drives to be told something is beautiful.
**Inputs** An ordinary-looking place; one verified fact wildly out of scale with its appearance; reachability; a resolution that requires presence.
**Outputs** One publishable place; one astonishment statement.
**Where it appears** Every place, every proposal, every video, every sign, every classroom.
**When NOT to use** Never absent. A place without it does not ship.
**Heart** §II, §III · **Manifesto** §4, §6, L3 · **Bible** §1 · **Rulebook** D-1, D-2 (both inviolable) · **Ecosystem** Part 1, Principle 9
**Design principles** Understate the appearance. Never inflate the fact. The gap does the work.
**Operational rules** All four conditions or reject. Exactly one astonishment — not zero, not three.
**Success signals** A stranger reacts before finishing the sentence.
**Failure signals** The reaction is *"that's pretty."*
**Metrics** Cold-reader reaction rate; rejection rate at authoring (a quarter with zero rejections means the gate is not being applied).
**Common mistakes** Publishing a famous place because it performs; adding a second fact to strengthen a weak first one.
**Examples** A limestone ceiling with two openings that people have stood beneath for longer than there are records. Six-and-a-half-thousand-year-old graves facing east behind a dune.
**Related** A2, B1, F1, F3

---

### A2 · THE RETELLABLE SENTENCE

**Purpose** Give every visitor something they can say to another human being.
**Human situation** Someone had a good day and cannot explain why, so the story dies with them.
**Trigger** Authoring a place; and the moment of departure.
**Desired emotional transition** Private satisfaction → transmissible story.
**Problem solved** A visitor who cannot retell it is a dead end, however happy they are.
**Inputs** The astonishment, compressed to one repeatable line.
**Outputs** One sentence per place, unchanged across every surface in the ecosystem.
**Where it appears** Search snippets, captions, a receptionist's mouth, a classroom, a message to one friend, a printed card, a plaque.
**When NOT to use** Never absent.
**Heart** §VI · **Manifesto** §8, L37 · **Bible** §11, L41 · **Rulebook** C-1 · **Ecosystem** Parts 1, 5, 10
**Design principles** Short enough to repeat from memory an hour later. Contains the disproportion. Contains no brand.
**Operational rules** Publication gate. Test: read to five people, ask them to repeat it an hour later.
**Success signals** It comes back to you from a stranger who has never used anything you built.
**Failure signals** People say *"there's this app…"* instead of the fact.
**Metrics** Unaided recall at one hour; share-to-one-person rate.
**Common mistakes** Writing a tagline. Writing something beautiful instead of something repeatable.
**Related** A1, C2, E1, E3

## GROUP B — DISCOVERY PATTERNS
*How a fact reaches someone who was not looking for one. Four patterns absorb twelve candidates.*

---

### B1 · THE SNAG

**Purpose** Interrupt an idle mind with a fact and a distance.
**Human situation** Sunday evening. Scrolling, driving, waiting. No intent.
**Trigger** Any ambient surface — a video, a feed, a sign, a passing remark, an open app.
**Desired emotional transition** Idleness → involuntary *"wait, what?"* → *"that can't be near me."*
**Problem solved** Demand cannot form around something nobody has heard of.
**Inputs** One astonishment; one location; one distance from this person.
**Outputs** A specific place lodged in a specific mind.
**Where it appears** Social video, feeds, road signs, the app's opening, ambient conversation.
**When NOT to use** When the person has already declared an intent — then use B3 or C1.
**Heart** §I, §II · **Manifesto** §2, §4 · **Bible** §2 (states 1–3), §4 · **Rulebook** H-1, H-4 · **Ecosystem** Part 2 Intent A
**Design principles** Fact before beauty. Distance in the same breath. No explanation of what we are.
**Operational rules** Involuntary reaction within ten seconds. Never open with an image alone.
**Success signals** They say it out loud without deciding to.
**Failure signals** Admiration, a like, and no movement.
**Metrics** Time-to-first-reaction; proposal→place-open rate.
**Common mistakes** Leading with a drone shot. Leading with the product.
**Related** A1, B2, C1

---

### B2 · THE HANDED SENTENCE

**Purpose** Let a trusted human deliver the astonishment on our behalf.
**Human situation** Someone asks a person they trust what to do tomorrow.
**Trigger** A guest at reception, a child at dinner, a colleague, a class, a guide.
**Desired emotional transition** Uncertainty → a specific plan, endorsed by someone real.
**Problem solved** No digital surface outperforms a person saying *go here.*
**Inputs** Three places within thirty minutes; one sentence each; honest costs; a reason for the teller to say it.
**Outputs** A journey we never touched.
**Where it appears** Hotels, guesthouses, schools, museums, guides, tourist centres, families, neighbours.
**When NOT to use** Never replace it with automation. The value is the human.
**Heart** §VI · **Manifesto** §1 · **Bible** §11 · **Rulebook** D-7 · **Ecosystem** Parts 2, 6, 7, Principles 14–16
**Design principles** Give people sentences, not portals. Make the teller look knowledgeable.
**Operational rules** Every partner receives ≤3 sentences with honest costs. No login, no dashboard.
**Success signals** A receptionist recites it from memory. A teacher uses it twice.
**Failure signals** Partners hold brochures they never mention.
**Metrics** Partner-attributed arrivals; sentence recall among partner staff.
**Common mistakes** Building a partner portal. Paying for placement instead of earning repetition.
**Related** A2, E3, E4, E5

---

### B3 · THE OCCUPIED SURFACE

**Purpose** Be correct, quotable and findable on ground we do not own.
**Human situation** Someone asks a question of a search box, a map, or a model — not of us.
**Trigger** *"what to do near ___"*, *"with kids"*, *"day trip from ___"*, a pin tapped on a map.
**Desired emotional transition** Open-ended search → one specific place with a cost.
**Problem solved** Most planning happens where we have no presence.
**Inputs** Structured, sourced, attributable facts; correct hours, access and coordinates.
**Outputs** An answer that arrives with a distance attached.
**Where it appears** Search, AI assistants, map platforms, forums, blogs.
**When NOT to use** Never as persuasion — these surfaces answer, they do not sell.
**Manifesto** §2 · **Ecosystem** Part 2 Intent B, Part 4, Principle 18
**Design principles** Answer the question they typed, then propose one place. Be the most accurate source about each name.
**Operational rules** Every place exists on the dominant map platform with correct hours, access notes and photographs.
**Success signals** A model quotes us. A map pin has our hours and they are right.
**Failure signals** Invisible under the words a normal person uses.
**Metrics** Name-search share; map-listing completeness; citation rate in AI answers.
**Common mistakes** Optimising for brand terms nobody types.
**Related** A2, C1, H1

---

### B4 · THE STANDALONE ENCOUNTER

**Purpose** Deliver complete value with no install, no signal and no account.
**Human situation** A person is standing at a place right now, wondering what they are looking at.
**Trigger** A QR at a site, a sign, a card, a printed page, a plaque.
**Desired emotional transition** Mild confusion → astonishment, on the spot.
**Problem solved** The most receptive person in the ecosystem currently has no way in.
**Inputs** One sentence. Nothing else.
**Outputs** An astonished person who owes us nothing.
**Where it appears** Site entrances, museums, guesthouses, tourist centres, print.
**When NOT to use** Never as an acquisition funnel. Value first, unconditionally.
**Heart** §V · **Ecosystem** Part 2 Intent C, Principles 5–6
**Design principles** Complete in itself. No install. No gate. No email.
**Operational rules** A QR that opens an app store is a failure and must be removed.
**Success signals** Someone reads a sign, drives there, and never contacts us. **That is a success, not a leak.**
**Failure signals** The first thing offered is a download.
**Metrics** Scan-to-read completion; site-adjacent arrivals.
**Common mistakes** Treating a physical touchpoint as a lead-capture device.
**Related** A2, B2, G2, H2

## GROUP C — DECISION PATTERNS
*How a fact becomes a Saturday. Three patterns absorb six candidates.*

---

### C1 · THE HONEST COST

**Purpose** Answer the feasibility question before it is asked.
**Human situation** Someone wants to go and does not know whether they can.
**Trigger** Any proposal, anywhere, on any surface.
**Desired emotional transition** Interest → *"we could actually do this on Saturday."*
**Problem solved** **The largest leak in the whole system** — silence about cost, and it is a content problem, not a product problem.
**Inputs** Distance from this person; realistic duration; difficulty; viable season; opening hours; child viability.
**Outputs** A decidable proposition.
**Where it appears** Every proposal on every surface, including a sentence spoken by a receptionist.
**When NOT to use** Never omitted. Unknowns are shown as unknown, never hidden.
**Manifesto** §1 · **Rulebook** D-3, D-4 · **Ecosystem** Part 5, Principles 10–11
**Design principles** In the same breath as the astonishment. Honest about difficulty even when it costs a visit.
**Operational rules** Six fields or it does not publish. *Unknown* is displayed, not concealed.
**Success signals** Departure times get set without further research.
**Failure signals** *"We'll do it when we have more time."*
**Metrics** Proposal→date-set rate; abandonment after opening a place.
**Common mistakes** Understating difficulty. Omitting hours. Hiding that the last kilometre is unpaved.
**Related** A1, C2, C3, G4

---

### C2 · THE SAID ALOUD

**Purpose** Convert a private interest into a shared commitment.
**Human situation** One person is interested; the trip requires at least one other.
**Trigger** The moment a proposal becomes concrete enough to repeat.
**Desired emotional transition** *"That's interesting"* → *"We should go Saturday"* — spoken to another person.
**Problem solved** Journeys are social. A decision held privately usually dies. **This transition is where the product wins or loses, and it happens away from every surface we own.**
**Inputs** A retellable sentence; an honest cost; a reason the second person should care.
**Outputs** A declared intention. Past the point of no return.
**Where it appears** Kitchens, cars, messages to one person, staff rooms.
**When NOT to use** Never engineer it with prompts. Make it easy and then stay out.
**Heart** §VI · **Bible** §2 (state 4–5), §11 · **Ecosystem** Part 5, Principle 12
**Design principles** Optimise for one-to-one, never one-to-many. The unit is a message to a single person.
**Operational rules** No feed, no followers, no public profile, no ranking.
**Success signals** Sent to exactly one person, with a reason attached.
**Failure signals** Broadcast to nobody in particular.
**Metrics** Share-to-single-recipient rate.
**Common mistakes** Building social features. The absence of them is the design.
**Related** A2, C1, E2, E6

---

### C3 · THE REDIRECT

**Purpose** Save a journey when the plan breaks.
**Human situation** It is raining, the road is closed, the site is shut, someone is ill.
**Trigger** Weather, closure, seasonality, a late start.
**Desired emotional transition** Deflation → *"then let's do the other one instead."*
**Problem solved** A broken plan currently ends the journey rather than moving it.
**Inputs** The original intention; conditions; the nearest equivalent that works today.
**Outputs** A different journey, taken.
**Where it appears** The morning of; the car; the last hour before departure.
**When NOT to use** Never to upsell. A redirect is a rescue, not an opportunity.
**Ecosystem** Part 5, Part 12 item 10
**Design principles** Equivalent, not lesser. Same day, same people, same appetite.
**Operational rules** Every place carries at least one weather-independent or season-shifted alternative within reach.
**Success signals** Rained-out Saturdays still produce journeys.
**Failure signals** Cancellations, silently.
**Metrics** Journeys taken on adverse-weather days.
**Common mistakes** Offering nothing. Offering something obviously worse.
**Related** C1, G3, G4

## GROUP D — JOURNEY PATTERNS
*From leaving the house to the first look. Four patterns absorb ten candidates.*

---

### D1 · THE QUIET APPROACH

**Purpose** Increase anticipation by subtraction and remove doubt at the threshold.
**Human situation** In the car. Bored, then rising. The road narrows and the signs stop.
**Trigger** Departure; then the final ten to fifteen minutes.
**Desired emotional transition** Transit → anticipation → certainty that this is the right road.
**Problem solved** Doubt at the threshold turns more cars around than distance ever has, and nothing measures it.
**Inputs** Something for the car to argue about in the long middle; silence on approach; one reassurance in the last kilometre.
**Outputs** A car that arrives confident and unspoiled.
**Where it appears** The whole drive out.
**When NOT to use** Never narrate the story in the car — a story spent in a moving vehicle cannot be spent at the place.
**Bible** §6, L29–L35 · **Rulebook** S-1
**Design principles** We get quieter as everything else gets louder. The passenger is a user.
**Operational rules** No story before arrival. Last kilometre: reassurance only.
**Success signals** Nobody turns around. Nobody arrives already knowing.
**Failure signals** The car has heard the punchline before it parks.
**Metrics** Approach-abandonment rate.
**Common mistakes** Filling the drive with content because the drive is long.
**Related** C1, D2, F5

---

### D2 · THE WITHDRAWAL

**Purpose** Get out of the way, correctly, on a schedule.
**Human situation** Doors, bags, the lock, the toilet question. Stiff from the car. Nobody feels awe at second five — they feel awkward.
**Trigger** Arrival detected.
**Desired emotional transition** Practical fog → orientation → release.
**Problem solved** Products interrupt attention at the exact second it should be handed to the place.
**Inputs** Confirmation that this is the place; the direction to walk.
**Outputs** A person walking toward something, unaccompanied.
**Where it appears** Every arrival, every scale, every surface.
**When NOT to use** Never skipped, never extended.
**Heart** §III · **Bible** §7, L11–L14 · **Rulebook** A-1, A-2, A-7, A-8 (inviolable)
**Design principles** One sentence, then nothing. Arrival is a threshold, not an achievement.
**Operational rules** ≤1 message in the first thirty seconds; zero output thereafter until the look has happened. No reward, sound, haptic or notification on arrival.
**Success signals** Phones go into pockets.
**Failure signals** Anyone reading anything while walking in.
**Metrics** Output events in protected windows (target: zero); screen-active share of dwell (<25%).
**Common mistakes** Auto-launching content on geofence trigger — the single most damaging habit available.
**Related** D1, D3, D4

---

### D3 · THE UNACCOMPANIED LOOK

**Purpose** Protect the first sight from being overwritten.
**Human situation** The place becomes visible, or closes around them, for the first time.
**Trigger** Crossing the registered threshold.
**Desired emotional transition** Anticipation → unmediated encounter.
**Problem solved** Narrate someone's first look and they will remember your sentence instead of what they saw — permanently.
**Inputs** A threshold, surveyed: position and bearing.
**Outputs** A memory that belongs to them.
**Where it appears** Every place, every scale — a cave mouth, a museum doorway, a ridge line.
**When NOT to use** Never violated. Safety is the only interruption.
**Bible** §7, L3, L4, L16 · **Rulebook** A-3, A-4, AR-1, AR-2 (inviolable)
**Design principles** Nothing mediates a first look. Not words, not a camera layer, not a reconstruction.
**Operational rules** No place publishes without a registered threshold. No technology activates before the look.
**Success signals** Someone stops walking without being told to.
**Failure signals** A person meets the place through a screen.
**Metrics** Mediation events before threshold+gap (target: zero).
**Common mistakes** Putting the most impressive capability at the most impressive moment.
**Related** D2, D4, F4

---

### D4 · THE HELD SILENCE

**Purpose** Give meaning room to land.
**Human situation** Something just happened, or is about to, and the person needs time they will not ask for.
**Trigger** After the first look; after every sentence; at the moment; on departure; on the drive home; in the weeks between journeys.
**Desired emotional transition** Stimulus → integration.
**Problem solved** Meaning happens in the pause, not in the sentence. Filling quiet is the most dangerous instinct on any team.
**Inputs** A defined duration and a defended boundary.
**Outputs** Attention that has settled enough to form its own question.
**Where it appears** Six places: the walk in; the gap after the first look; after every narration; the moment; the first miles home; the weeks of no contact.
**When NOT to use** Never shortened for engagement. Safety only.
**Bible** §15 in full, L2 · **Rulebook** S-1, S-2, N-2 (inviolable), S-4
**Design principles** Longer than feels comfortable. Boredom at a great place is the beginning of attention, not a failure.
**Operational rules** ≥30 s after the first look before any content. Non-zero trailing silence after every segment. No next proposal on the day of a visit. No music on site, ever.
**Success signals** People start talking to each other instead of to a device.
**Failure signals** A team member proposes filling a silence with something.
**Metrics** Output events per silence window; broken-versus-intentional perception in field study.
**Common mistakes** Solving *"is it broken?"* by adding content. Solve it another way.
**Related** D2, D3, F2, F4

## GROUP E — PLACE AND AFTER PATTERNS
*What happens on the ground and afterwards. Seven patterns absorb eighteen candidates.*

---

### F1 · THE POINTING

**Purpose** Make ground readable.
**Human situation** Standing in a field, seeing a field.
**Trigger** After the gap, when the person's own question has formed.
**Desired emotional transition** Looking → seeing.
**Problem solved** A place cannot be told; it can only be pointed at.
**Inputs** One idea; one thing visible from where they stand.
**Outputs** Eyes that move. A field that has become a scene.
**Where it appears** On site only.
**When NOT to use** If it works equally well at home, it belongs in the planning stage, not here.
**Manifesto** §8 · **Bible** L32 · **Rulebook** N-3, N-4, N-7
**Design principles** Specific, not beautiful. The first sentence points; it does not perform.
**Operational rules** Named visible referent per segment. Segments ≤90 s. Interpretation marked as interpretation in the moment, not in a footnote.
**Success signals** The listener turns around.
**Failure signals** Lyrical writing that produces no movement.
**Metrics** Observed head and body movement during narration.
**Common mistakes** Opening with the most beautiful line instead of the most specific one.
**Related** D4, F2, F3, F6

---

### F2 · ONE IDEA AT A TIME

**Purpose** Stay under the absorption limit.
**Human situation** Outdoors, standing, with family, in weather. Attention is expensive and shared.
**Trigger** Every stop within a place.
**Desired emotional transition** Curiosity → satisfaction → capacity for the next thing.
**Problem solved** Everything a place *could* say competes with the place itself.
**Inputs** One idea. Three to five stops.
**Outputs** A visit that is remembered rather than endured.
**When NOT to use** Never exceeded because the research was interesting.
**Bible** §8 · **Rulebook** P-1, P-2, C-2, AT-2
**Design principles** Less than they can absorb. A stop whose summary needs the word *and* is two stops.
**Operational rules** ≤3 supporting facts per place. ≤2 required interactions per stop.
**Success signals** People still have attention at the last stop.
**Failure signals** The visit becomes a lecture with walking.
**Metrics** Stop-completion distribution; screen-active share.
**Common mistakes** Adding a sixth stop.
**Related** F1, F3, G1

---

### F3 · THE UNANSWERED QUESTION

**Purpose** Send people away still holding something.
**Human situation** A visit ends and everything has been resolved.
**Trigger** Departure.
**Desired emotional transition** Satisfaction → productive incompleteness.
**Problem solved** A visitor who leaves fully informed has *closed* the place. One who leaves with a question keeps it — and passes it on as a question, which is the most contagious form there is.
**Inputs** One deliberately unresolved thread, recorded at authoring.
**Outputs** A person who brings it up again days later.
**When NOT to use** Never as a trick or a cliffhanger. It must be a real open question.
**Bible** §8 · **Rulebook** P-4
**Design principles** Genuinely open. Not withheld information — unresolved meaning.
**Success signals** People can state it in an exit interview.
**Failure signals** Content that answers everything it raises.
**Metrics** Unaided recall of the question.
**Related** A2, F1, H1

---

### F4 · THE AFTERWORD

**Purpose** Deepen a relationship that already exists with a place.
**Human situation** They have seen it with their own eyes and now want to know more.
**Trigger** After the unmediated look, on request only.
**Desired emotional transition** *"I saw it"* → *"so that's what I was looking at."*
**Problem solved** Any layer shown first replaces the place; shown last it deepens it.
**Inputs** An additional layer — a reconstruction, an overlay, a recording, a diagram, an expert, a technology not yet invented.
**Outputs** Understanding, added to an intact memory.
**Where it appears** Anywhere a richer layer exists.
**When NOT to use** **Never before the first look. Never as the moment itself. Never as the reason anyone came.**
**Bible** §7, §9, L15 · **Rulebook** AR-1, AR-2, AR-4 (inviolable)
**Design principles** *Guardian Sight, augmented reality, audio reconstruction and everything that replaces them in twenty years are all instances of this pattern.* **The pattern survives; the technology does not.**
**Operational rules** Complete alternative always exists (see G3). Nothing exists only in the enhanced layer.
**Success signals** People use it after, and would have been fine without it.
**Failure signals** It becomes the reason for the visit, or the peak of it.
**Metrics** Activation timestamps relative to the threshold; completion rate with the layer disabled.
**Common mistakes** Building the most expensive thing and then needing it to matter.
**Related** D3, D4, G3

---

### F5 · THE SECOND PERSON

**Purpose** Design for whoever did not choose to come.
**Human situation** A car with four people. One decided. Three came.
**Trigger** Every journey involving more than one person — which is most of them.
**Desired emotional transition** Passivity → participation.
**Problem solved** The decider is served by everything; the passenger, the child and the reluctant partner are served by nothing.
**Inputs** Something the passenger can read aloud; something a child can find first; honest child viability; somewhere to sit.
**Outputs** A journey remembered by everyone in the car, and a child who asks to go again.
**When NOT to use** Never. Even solo journeys are usually retold to someone.
**Bible** §6, L17, L30 · **Rulebook** G-10 · **Ecosystem** Principles 12–13
**Design principles** The child is a distribution mechanism, not an audience. Write for the person reading aloud to a driver who cannot look.
**Operational rules** Child viability stated honestly, including when the answer is no.
**Success signals** The eight-year-old finds the path first and tells their class on Monday.
**Failure signals** One person engaged, three bored.
**Metrics** Repeat rate among family groups; school-derived family arrivals.
**Related** B2, C2, E4

---

### F6 · THE EVIDENCE

**Purpose** Give a day something to be remembered by.
**Human situation** Years later, someone wants to find a feeling again.
**Trigger** Departure — never at the moment.
**Desired emotional transition** Experience → retrievable memory.
**Problem solved** Memory needs an index; but anything we record at the moment is a moment we took.
**Inputs** Where, when, who with, what the weather did.
**Outputs** Proof that a day happened.
**When NOT to use** Never at the moment. Never obtainable without going. Never rare, tiered, tradeable or expiring.
**Manifesto** §9, L38–L41 · **Bible** L22 · **Rulebook** PR-6, PR-7, MEM-1, MEM-3 (inviolable)
**Design principles** One object per place, not four nested tiers. Organised the way memory is, not the way our structure is.
**Operational rules** Name the physical act that produced it. No act, no object. No selling inside a memory surface.
**Success signals** Someone opens it years later for no reason.
**Failure signals** It becomes a score, a set, or a completion target.
**Metrics** Archive revisit rate at 6+ months.
**Common mistakes** Building five reward tiers for someone who has been to one place.
**Related** F3, F7, G1

---

### F7 · THE SECOND LOOK

**Purpose** Produce infinite journeys from a finite catalogue.
**Human situation** Everything within two hours has been done.
**Trigger** Season, weather, hour, anniversary, a life change.
**Desired emotional transition** *"Finished"* → *"I have never seen it like this."*
**Problem solved** The weekend loop has a real ceiling, and more places is the expensive answer to it.
**Inputs** A known place; a condition that makes it a different place.
**Outputs** A journey to somewhere already visited.
**When NOT to use** Never as filler for an absent proposal.
**Bible** §13 · **Rulebook** G-12 · **Ecosystem** Part 6, Principle 28
**Design principles** Places are not consumed. Snow, dawn, harvest, flood, the month it means something.
**Operational rules** Every place ships with a named second-visit proposition.
**Success signals** Return visits to places marked complete.
**Failure signals** Users report running out.
**Metrics** Repeat-visit rate per place; off-season journeys.
**Related** C3, F6, G4

## GROUP G — STEWARDSHIP PATTERNS
*Obligations to the places and the people who live beside them. Three patterns.*

---

### E1 · THE CUSTODIAN'S VERSION

**Purpose** Let the people who hold a place tell it.
**Human situation** A place has a local meaning that no archive contains.
**Trigger** Authoring any inhabited, sacred or actively used place.
**Desired emotional transition** Researched → witnessed.
**Problem solved** The best material is held by people, and gathering it is simultaneously the research method and the consent mechanism.
**Inputs** The local historian, the priest, the woman who has lived beside it for sixty years.
**Outputs** Material nobody else can obtain, and permission to use it.
**When NOT to use** Never skipped where a community exists.
**Heart** §VIII · **Rulebook** C-3, C-4 (inviolable) · **Ecosystem** Parts 7, 8, Principles 21–22
**Design principles** Their version outranks ours. Nothing invented, ever.
**Operational rules** Non-objection precedes publication. Attribution recorded and preserved.
**Success signals** The community treats the account as theirs.
**Failure signals** A community that feels extracted from — and can close a site permanently.
**Metrics** Places with recorded consent; community-originated corrections.
**Related** E2, F1, H2

---

### E2 · THE BENEFICIARY

**Purpose** Make sure the place gains before it loses.
**Human situation** Strangers begin arriving somewhere quiet.
**Trigger** The first sustained increase in visitors.
**Desired emotional transition** Local indifference or suspicion → participation.
**Problem solved** Success and damage arrive by the same road, in the same cars. Overtourism is the standard failure mode of exactly this kind of product.
**Inputs** Footfall; a coffee stand; a guesthouse; a municipality that can fund access.
**Outputs** A place more able to receive the next arrival than before we sent the first.
**When NOT to use** Never optional. If we cannot meet it somewhere, we do not go there.
**Heart** §VIII · **Ecosystem** Parts 6, 7, 10, Principles 23–25
**Design principles** Volume is never the goal. Refusing to publish is a legitimate outcome.
**Operational rules** Fragility outranks demand. Make value visible to municipalities in numbers they recognise.
**Success signals** Locals defend the arrangement.
**Failure signals** Degradation; restricted access; resentment.
**Metrics** Local revenue signals; site condition over time; access changes.
**Related** E1, E3, H2

---

### E3 · THE TRUST LEDGER

**Purpose** Treat credibility as a spendable balance.
**Human situation** Someone has taken one of our recommendations and is deciding whether to take another.
**Trigger** Every recommendation.
**Desired emotional transition** *"Worth a try"* → *"whatever they say, we go."*
**Problem solved** A mediocre place costs the next three journeys, and the person never tells us why they stopped.
**Inputs** Every published place; every proposal.
**Outputs** Either compounding belief or silent attrition.
**When NOT to use** Never suspended for a partner, a deadline, or a gap on the map.
**Manifesto** §6, L8–L9 · **Rulebook** D-6 (inviolable) · **Ecosystem** Part 10
**Design principles** No filler, ever. A gap is honest; filler is a lie that takes months to detect.
**Operational rules** Every publication answers *what would be lost if this did not exist?* — *"the set would be incomplete"* is a rejection.
**Success signals** People go without reading the details.
**Failure signals** Quiet disengagement with no complaint.
**Metrics** Second-recommendation acceptance rate.
**Related** A1, C1, E2

## GROUP H — SYSTEM PATTERNS
*How the product behaves as a whole. Three patterns absorb ten candidates.*

---

### G1 · THE EARNED REVEAL

**Purpose** Show nothing before it can be wanted.
**Human situation** A newcomer and a five-year veteran using the same thing.
**Trigger** Accumulated journeys.
**Desired emotional transition** *"There's more here"* → *"these weren't random"* → *"I understand the shape of it."*
**Problem solved** Scale shown early is a boast; the same scale shown later is a gift.
**Inputs** Depth, structure, systems, history, long arcs.
**Outputs** Complexity that arrives as reward rather than tax.
**When NOT to use** Never to withhold something the person needs to decide — that is C1's territory.
**Manifesto** §12, §13 · **Rulebook** H-3, PR-1, PR-3, PR-4 (inviolable) · **Ecosystem** Principle 32
**Design principles** Surface area does not grow with content. Depth lives one level down. Never render absence.
**Operational rules** No global completion display. No zero states. Nothing shows what is missing.
**Success signals** Veterans find depth; newcomers never meet it.
**Failure signals** A first-time user meeting the whole structure.
**Metrics** Decision count on primary surfaces across catalogue growth (target: constant).
**Related** F2, F6, F7

---

### G2 · THE ASSUMED ABSENCE

**Purpose** Work where the good places are.
**Human situation** No signal, 30% battery, cold fingers, direct sun, tired, one hand, family waiting.
**Trigger** Every on-site moment.
**Desired emotional transition** Anxiety about the tool → forgetting it exists.
**Problem solved** Everything worth going to is somewhere the network is not.
**Inputs** Content in hand before departure; graceful degradation; no dependency on connection.
**Outputs** A tool that never becomes the problem.
**When NOT to use** Never assume connectivity, anywhere, for anything on site.
**Manifesto** §11, L18 · **Rulebook** AT-6, AR-5 (inviolable), P-7
**Design principles** Degrade silently. Never explain what someone is missing. Never show an error that implies breakage.
**Operational rules** Full field-conditions test each release. Any visit interruptible and resumable at zero cost.
**Success signals** Nobody notices the network.
**Failure signals** A modal apologising for a device.
**Metrics** Field-test sign-off; offline completion rate.
**Related** B4, D2, G3

---

### G3 · THE COMPLETE ALTERNATIVE

**Purpose** Guarantee that no one is locked out of anything that matters.
**Human situation** A person who cannot see well, hear well, walk far, hold a camera up, read small text, or afford a modern device.
**Trigger** Every piece of content and every enhanced layer.
**Desired emotional transition** Exclusion → full participation.
**Problem solved** Accessibility is the difference between a national memory and a private club.
**Inputs** Every path, doubled: audio and text, enhanced and plain, long walk and short.
**Outputs** One experience, reachable several ways.
**When NOT to use** Never. This is the pattern most often quietly dropped under deadline.
**Heart** §VII · **Rulebook** AR-4, G-6 (inviolable) · **Manifesto** §11
**Design principles** Nothing exists only in one modality. An accessible route is *designed*, not defaulted to — and it must still deliver the moment.
**Operational rules** Every place completes end-to-end with the enhanced layer disabled. Readable in direct sun.
**Success signals** A seventy-year-old finishes.
**Failure signals** The alternative exists but is worse.
**Metrics** Completion rate with enhancements disabled; accessibility sign-off per place.
**Related** F4, G2, E2

# PASS 5 — GOVERNANCE

Every feature request answers four questions before design begins:

1. **Which patterns does it use?** Name them. A feature using none is not a feature; it is a preference.
2. **Which patterns does it strengthen?** If none, it is at best neutral and is occupying capacity.
3. **Does it violate any pattern?** Check the conflicts column in Pass 3 first — the five standing tensions account for most violations.
4. **Can it be built without a new pattern?** Almost always yes.

**The bar for a new pattern** — all four required:
- A human behaviour that recurs across at least three unrelated contexts.
- A product response that recurs with it.
- It cannot be expressed as an existing pattern with different inputs.
- It survives the country test below.

**Anything failing the bar is an instance, not a pattern.** Instances are welcome; they simply do not get named here. Guardian Sight is an instance. So is any successor technology, any single partner programme, and any specific screen.

# THE FINAL TESTS

## Could a new designer build with only this document?

Yes, with one gap they must be told about: **this library describes behaviour, not tone.** A designer using only these twenty-six patterns would build something structurally identical to Unlocking Bulgaria — silent at the right moments, honest about cost, never rewarding arrival, never ranking anyone. To also make it *sound* like Unlocking Bulgaria they need one thing more: read the arrival chapter of the Experience Bible once. That is the only piece of craft in the entire canon that cannot be compressed into a pattern.

## If Unlocking Bulgaria became Unlocking Europe tomorrow — which patterns survive?

**All twenty-six, unchanged.**

Not one of them contains a country, a place name, a story, a mechanic or a proper noun. That is not a happy accident; it is the test that decided what was allowed to become a pattern.

**What does not survive is everything the project currently names most proudly:** the Keeper, the Предели, the Master Keys, the Living Covenant, Guardian Sight, the four-key formula, the sixty-four checkpoints. Every one of them is an instance — a specific, valuable, replaceable expression of a pattern underneath.

> **The patterns are the product. The lore is the implementation.**

That sentence is uncomfortable, and it is the most useful thing in this document.
