# PRODUCT MANIFESTO

*THE_HEART_OF_THE_PROJECT states why we exist. It does not change. This document states how the product must behave in order to be an honest expression of it. The product will be rebuilt many times; this should survive each rebuild.*

*It contains no features and no implementation. It is written to settle arguments. If a proposal cannot be defended with a chapter or a law from this document, the proposal is not ready.*

*Every chapter answers one question: how does this move someone closer to leaving home?*

---

## 1. THE PRODUCT PROMISE

We do not promise an experience. We make a proposal.

The practical promise, from the first session onward, is this: **you will learn about a real place you did not know existed yesterday, it will be close enough for you to reach, and you will know what it would take to go.**

Three parts, all required, none optional:

**Specific.** Not a region, not a theme, not a list. One place with a name and a location.
**Reachable.** Weighed against where the person actually is, what day it is, and what they can realistically do. A magnificent place eight hours away is not a proposal — it is a poster.
**Costed.** How far, how long, how hard, what season, whether the children will manage. A proposal that hides its cost is not a proposal; it is an advertisement.

Two honest exclusions.

We do not promise to be entertaining at home. Any session that satisfies someone into staying put has worked against us. We would rather be slightly frustrating on the sofa and indispensable at the trailhead.

We do not promise completeness. We are not the record of a country. We are a series of well-made invitations.

→ *A person leaves home when the destination is specific, the cost is known, and the decision has already been made simple.*

---

## 2. THE FIRST FIVE MINUTES

The first five minutes have exactly one job: **move the person's attention off the product and onto a place.**

The moment we are aiming for is not *"this app is good."* It is *"wait — where is that?"* Everything in the opening either causes that sentence or delays it.

**What must disappear.** Accounts. Forms. Permission stacks presented as a wall. Tutorials. Feature tours. Anything that asks the person to evaluate us. Any request for a preference they do not yet have the information to hold. In the first five minutes we ask for nothing, because we have not yet earned the right to ask for anything.

**What must become visible.** One place. Ordinary-looking. Close. Attached to a single fact large enough to feel out of proportion with its appearance. And one way to say yes.

**What must remain hidden.** The scale of what we have built. The systems. The pricing. The larger story. Any counter reading zero. A new person shown how much exists does not feel excitement; they feel the weight of a syllabus, and they put it down. Scale is a reward, not an introduction.

The only first-session measure worth watching is the time between opening and the first involuntary *where is that*. Everything else in the first five minutes is a candidate for deletion.

→ *Nobody leaves home for a product. They leave home for a place, and five minutes is enough to hand them one.*

---

## 3. THE INVISIBLE PRODUCT

There is a class of thought a person should never have while using this, and it sounds like: *did that save? do I own this one? do I need signal? am I in the right place? was I supposed to do something first?*

That is bookkeeping. Bookkeeping is the tax a product charges for its own existence, and ours must approach zero.

The product should silently know, without ever asking: where the person is; whether they are planning or arriving; whether the weather makes today a bad idea; what they already have; what they already saw; what language they think in; whether they are alone or with people; whether the signal is about to vanish and content should already be in hand.

Two consequences worth naming.

**Planning and being-there are two different products, and the person must never switch between them manually.** Sitting at a kitchen table three weeks out and standing in a wind at the site require almost nothing in common. Context decides which one appears. Asking the user to declare their own mode is a design failure.

**Every state must be recoverable without effort.** Lost signal, dead battery, changed phone, a year away — the person returns and everything is simply where they left it. Nothing they earned by physically going anywhere may ever depend on them having managed it correctly.

→ *Doubt is friction. A person who is unsure whether the product is working does not get in the car.*

---

## 4. THE DISCOVERY ENGINE

Not maps. Not AI. Not AR. Not photography.

The engine is **disproportion**: an ordinary-looking place carrying one true fact far too large for it.

This distinction decides everything. A beautiful photograph of a famous place produces *admiration*, and admiration is satisfied at a distance — you look, you feel it, you are done, you never go. Disproportion produces *curiosity*, and curiosity cannot be satisfied remotely. It has to be walked into.

The formula, which every place must satisfy before it is published:

**Something that looks unremarkable + one true fact wildly out of scale with its appearance + it is reachable + the resolution requires presence.**

All four. Remove reachability and you have a documentary. Remove disproportion and you have a guidebook. Remove truth and you have nothing worth defending. Remove the requirement of presence — if the fact fully resolves in the telling — and you have entertained someone into staying home.

This makes our scarcest resource obvious, and it is not places, technology, or content volume. It is **the single irreducible astonishment attached to each location.** Finding it is the hardest, slowest, most valuable work anyone here does. A place without one is not a place we are ready to publish, however photogenic it is and however much of a gap it fills.

→ *Curiosity is the only force strong enough to get an adult to spend a Saturday on a stranger's suggestion.*

---

## 5. THE JOURNEY PHILOSOPHY

The journey is not the drive. It begins the moment someone hears and ends long after they are home.

Seven stages, and the product must be competent in all of them and dominant in none: **hearing · wanting · deciding · telling someone · going · being there · retelling.**

Most products are built for one stage and pretend the others do not exist. Ours must recognise that the decision usually happens days before the trip, on a sofa, with someone else in the room — and that the moment with the most emotional charge is often not the arrival but the drive home, when it is being talked about for the first time.

Three obligations follow.

**Be excellent three weeks early.** The planning moment is where journeys are actually won or lost, and it is almost always designed as an afterthought.

**Design for the return.** The hour after leaving a place is when a memory sets and when the next journey is cheapest to start. It is also when people are most willing to tell someone. A product that goes quiet on the way home has abandoned its best moment.

**Never make the journey feel like transit.** The distance between places is not dead time to be minimised. It is where anticipation is built and where the conversation happens.

→ *Leaving home is a decision made days earlier, by someone who is not alone.*

---

## 6. THE PLACE PHILOSOPHY

The bar for publishing a place is a single question:

**Strip away everything we added. Would a thoughtful person still feel their day was well spent?**

If not, no amount of story, atmosphere, or technology rescues it. We would only be dressing up a disappointment, and people can feel that within ninety seconds of arriving.

**Coverage is a vanity metric.** A country represented by forty extraordinary places is worth more than one represented by four hundred adequate ones — because we are not building a catalogue, we are building a reputation for the *next* recommendation.

This is the trust economy, and it should be understood as a balance sheet. Every recommendation spends credibility. An extraordinary place repays it several times over. A mediocre one costs us the next three journeys, and the person will never tell us why they stopped.

Therefore: **we never fill.** No place is added to complete a set, reach a number, cover a region, satisfy a partner, or round out a route. A gap on the map is honest. Filler is a lie that takes months to detect and years to recover from.

And a place is more than a location — it is a location *at a time*. Sending someone to the right place in the wrong season, wrong weather, or wrong hour is the same failure as sending them somewhere unworthy. We are responsible for when, not only where.

→ *People go back out because the last place we sent them to was worth it. That is the entire growth model.*

## 7. THE QUEST PHILOSOPHY

Quests do not exist to make this fun. They exist because of a fact about adults:

**"You should go see something interesting" is not an instruction anyone acts on. "Go here, and find out what happened" is.**

An open-ended possibility stays a possibility forever. A quest converts it into something with a beginning, an end, and a size that fits inside a day — which is the only shape most people can actually schedule.

A quest does two things and nothing else: it gives a **reason** to go and **permission** to spend the day that way. Grown adults need permission to do something purposeless; a quest supplies it.

If quests vanished tomorrow, people would browse beautiful things and go nowhere. That is the honest measure of what they are for.

**How they must feel.** Like an errand with a secret. Purposeful, light, unhurried, entirely optional, and — critically — like the person's own idea. Never like homework, never like a checklist, never like an obligation that follows someone home.

**The hard limit.** The quest must never become more interesting than the place. If someone is doing this for the structure rather than the ground, we have quietly built a game, and games are played indoors.

→ *A vague intention never becomes a Saturday. A specific errand does.*

---

## 8. THE STORY PHILOSOPHY

History's role is not education and not entertainment. Its role is to make ground **readable**.

Standing in a field, a person sees a field. Told what happened on it, they see a scene. That conversion is the entire function of story here, and it has a physical test: **the listener's eyes move.** They look up, turn, walk twenty metres to see it from where it happened. If nothing in the body responds, the story failed, however well written it was.

From that, four rules.

**Story is addressed to the location.** It must point at something the listener can actually see. Any passage that works exactly as well at home does not belong on site — it belongs in the planning stage, doing a different job.

**Truth is not negotiable; interpretation announces itself.** We can say what is known, and we can say what it might mean, and a person must always be able to tell which one they just heard. We are working with real graves and living communities. Nothing here gets decorated.

**Retellable beats complete.** One true thing that survives the drive home is worth more than twelve accurate things that do not. Every story should hand someone a sentence they can say to another human being. That sentence is how places outlive us.

**Emotion yes, sentimentality no.** Weight, silence, and restraint move people. Adjectives do not.

→ *A person will drive three hours for a story, and no distance at all for a fact.*

---

## 9. THE COLLECTION PHILOSOPHY

People collect because collection is memory with an index. Nobody wants the object. They want a reliable way back to a feeling, and a shape that proves the feeling happened.

So what we give must be **evidence of having been somewhere** — never a reward for having used something. The distinction is total.

**Never collectible:** anything obtainable without going. Anything completable at home. Anything with rarity, tiers, or trading. Anything with an expiry. Anything that could induce someone to visit a place they do not care about in order to close a set — the moment that becomes possible, the collection has started corrupting the journey it was meant to record.

**The test:** if the object is more interesting than the place, delete the object.

And the archive's real purpose is the look back, years later — so it must be organised the way memory is: when, who with, what the weather did, what happened. Never by our internal structure. The person is not browsing our system. They are looking for a day.

→ *Nothing here is worth having unless it was collected somewhere real, which means the only way to want more of it is to go.*

---

## 10. THE PROGRESSION PHILOSOPHY

Progression is an emotional sequence, not a counter. These are the states we are responsible for producing.

**After one place** — *"That was real. I want to tell someone."* Nothing about percentages. Nothing about what remains. The only correct feeling after one is the urge to talk.

**After five** — *"I'm someone who does this now."* An identity forms. They also begin to sense that the choices are not random — that whoever picked these has taste — and that suspicion is what makes the sixth recommendation land.

**After twenty** — *"I know this country differently from other people."* Earned expertise. This is when they begin recommending without being asked, which is when the product starts working through people instead of screens.

**After fifty** — *"I am responsible for this."* And, honestly, a note of grief at approaching the end. At this point we owe them something to do that is not consuming more: bringing someone else, going deeper into places they already know, standing somewhere in a season they have never seen it.

One law governs all of it: **progress must read as abundance, never as debt.**

Most progression systems manufacture the feeling of being behind, because being behind drives returns. Ours must produce the opposite — a person with a full life should feel invited by everything they have not done, never indebted by it. We never show someone what they are missing. Completion is not the goal; the next place is.

→ *Debt makes people avoid a product. Abundance makes them plan a trip.*

---

## 11. THE INTERFACE PHILOSOPHY

"The interface disappears" is not a visual style. It is a measurable claim about decisions.

**On site, the correct amount of interface approaches none.** Something to begin, something to stop. Nothing to browse, nothing to configure, nothing to read while walking. The person's attention belongs to what is in front of them, and every element we place on that screen is competing with a cave wall.

**Physical context replaces navigation.** What appears should follow from where someone is, what time it is, what the weather is doing, and what they were last doing. A person should almost never have to traverse a structure to arrive at the thing they came for. Menus are what a product uses when it does not know where you are; we always know.

**The specific rule that matters most:** the last thing on the screen before the moment is nothing. We do not want to be present in the memory. The interface must be gone before the memory begins.

**Design conditions are non-negotiable:** one hand, direct sun, wind, cold fingers, thirty percent battery, no signal, someone tired at the end of a long drive with their family waiting. A design that only works in an office has not been tested.

→ *Every decision we ask for on site is attention taken from the reason they came.*

---

## 12. THE SIMPLICITY PRINCIPLE

The product will become infinitely rich. It must never become complicated. These are different axes, and confusing them is how good products die.

**The rule that makes it possible:** the surface area must not grow with the content. Whether there are ten places or ten thousand, the person faces the same small number of decisions. Growth happens in the world, not in the interface.

**Complexity is always one level down, never on the surface.** Depth is available to anyone who reaches for it and invisible to everyone who does not. The person who wants forty minutes of context and the person who wants one sentence must both be served without either being taxed by the other's needs.

**We never teach the product.** People here learn about places. If someone has to learn a system, we have made them a student of our machinery instead of a visitor to a country. Any onboarding that explains how the product works is a confession.

**Defaults are decisions we make so nobody else has to.** No setting exists that a well-chosen default could have decided. Every option we add is a small admission that we could not make up our minds and have passed the problem to a person standing in a field.

**The complexity budget belongs to the content, not the interface.** Spend it on the world.

→ *Complication produces hesitation, and hesitation is where a Saturday gets spent at home.*

---

## 13. PROGRESSIVE DISCLOSURE

Nothing is revealed before it can be desired. Scale shown early is a boast; the same scale shown later is a gift.

**Day one.** One place, one reason, one way to say yes. No systems, no structure, no pricing, no larger story, and no indication of how much exists. The person should finish their first session knowing about a place and almost nothing about us.

**Week one.** A second and third place — and the first faint sense that the selection has a logic. Not explained. Felt. This is when someone stops thinking they found an app and starts thinking they found a point of view.

**Month one.** Structure becomes visible, and it becomes visible because they went looking. Now the shape of the thing is a reward for interest rather than a demand made of a stranger. This is also when knowing that other people are out here doing the same thing becomes valuable rather than noisy.

**Year one.** The whole. The long arcs, the depth that only makes sense to someone who has stood in enough places, the things that cannot be understood any other way. And their own record — which by now is more interesting to them than anything we have planned next, because it is theirs.

→ *A person who is shown everything at once evaluates. A person who is shown one thing goes.*

## 14. THE PRODUCT LAWS

Fifty rules. They settle arguments.

**On the place**
1. The place is the hero. We are the supporting cast.
2. A place is never content.
3. One irreducible astonishment per place, or it does not ship.
4. Never fill a route to reach a number.
5. Coverage is a vanity metric.
6. A gap on the map is honest; filler is a lie.
7. Strip away everything we added — if the day is not worth it, do not send anyone.
8. A mediocre place costs us the next three journeys.
9. Trust is spent on every recommendation and earned back slowly.
10. Right place, wrong season is a failure, not a detail.

**On the person**
11. Never explain before you inspire.
12. Ask for nothing you can infer.
13. Ask for nothing until it is needed.
14. Never make someone do bookkeeping.
15. The user should never wonder whether it saved.
16. Nothing earned by physically going anywhere may be lost by mismanaging an account.
17. Design for the second person in the car.
18. Assume tired, cold, one hand, low battery, no signal.
19. Someone who goes once and never returns is a success, not churn.
20. Every place must be leaveable, with no penalty for stopping.

**On the screen**
21. Every screen creates curiosity or gets out of the way.
22. The interface disappears before the memory begins.
23. The last thing on screen before the moment is nothing.
24. Menus are what a product uses when it does not know where you are.
25. The number of decisions must not grow with the amount of content.
26. Depth lives one level down, never on the surface.
27. No setting exists that a good default could have decided.
28. Teach places, never teach the product.
29. Onboarding that explains the system has failed.
30. A design that only works in an office has not been tested.

**On the story**
31. If it works equally well at home, it does not belong on site.
32. The story must point at something the listener can see.
33. Retellable beats complete.
34. Truth is not negotiable; interpretation must announce itself.
35. Emotion is allowed. Sentimentality is not.
36. Adjectives are what we write when we failed to find the fact.
37. Hand every person one sentence they can say to someone else.

**On reward**
38. Nothing is collectible that can be obtained without going.
39. No rarity, no trading, no scarcity, no expiry.
40. If the object is more interesting than the place, delete the object.
41. The archive is organised by memory, not by our structure.
42. Progress reads as abundance, never as debt.
43. Never show someone what they are missing.
44. Real memories outrank virtual achievements.
45. Movement is rewarded more than interaction.

**On going and shipping**
46. Never interrupt an arrival. Never sell inside a moment.
47. Design for the drive home, not only for the arrival.
48. The best outcome is a journey we never see.
49. A feature that increases screen time and decreases road time has failed.
50. Technology is never the destination, and the app is always temporary.

## 15. THE ULTIMATE PRODUCT TEST

Before anything ships — a feature, a screen, a sentence, a partnership, a season of work — one question:

> ## Does this make it more likely that someone discovers, visits, and remembers another real place?

All three verbs. Discovery without a visit is entertainment. A visit without memory is tourism. Memory without another is an ending.

**The burden of proof sits with the proposal, not with the objection.** "It does no harm" is not a passing answer. Anything that cannot articulate which of the three verbs it strengthens is, at best, occupying room that something else should have.

When the answer is unclear, three follow-ups usually resolve it:

*Would this still matter if the person never opened it again after the trip?*
*Does it help the second person in the car?*
*If a competitor copied this exactly, would people go outside more?*

And when principles collide, the order is fixed:

**The place, then the person, then the journey, then the product.**

The product loses every argument it is in. That is what it is for.

*The Heart tells us why. This tells us how. Neither of them tells us what to build — that is the work, and it is never finished.*
