# THE HEART OF THE PROJECT

**Version 2**

*Nothing in this document depends on a product, a mechanic, a story, or a country. Every claim here must still be true if everything we have built disappears. When this document and any other document disagree, this one wins.*

---

## I. THE PERSON WE ARE FOR

Sometime in their twenties, most people stop finding things out.

Not on purpose. The map just stops growing. You learn the road to work, the road to your parents, the two or three places worth a weekend, and then for the next forty years you move along lines you have already drawn. The world does not shrink. Your version of it does.

And here is the part that should bother us: you can live your entire life forty kilometres from something that would have changed you, and no one will ever mention it. Not once. There is a cave in northwest Bulgaria with paintings on the wall made eight thousand years before Christ. Millions of people have driven past the turnoff. Nobody told them.

Meanwhile we have never been so thoroughly informed and so rarely astonished. We have seen photographs of everything and stood in front of almost nothing. This is the quiet damage of the last twenty years: **the image has become a vaccine against the place.** You have seen the picture, so you feel you have been. The itch is scratched. You never go.

So the person we are for is not bored and not uneducated. They are something worse and much more common.

They are a curious adult who has run out of first times.

---

## II. WHY THIS MUST EXIST

The reflex is to say the problem is access. It isn't. Everything is findable. Every ruin, every gorge, every painted wall has a page, a pin, a photograph, a set of coordinates.

Availability solved nothing.

Look at what is actually pointed at people. Guidebooks send you where everyone already goes, because that is what a guidebook can safely recommend. Algorithms send you where everyone already went, because that is the only thing they can measure. Search answers the question you already thought to ask. Every one of these systems is downstream of demand — and demand cannot form around something you have never heard of.

Which leaves a specific, unclaimed job:

> **Nothing in the world is built to make a person go somewhere they had never heard of yesterday.**

That is the gap. Not information. Not access. *Ignition.* The distance between knowing a place exists and being in the car.

We exist to close that distance, one person and one place at a time. If someone else closed it better tomorrow, we should be glad and go home.

We begin in Bulgaria — not because the argument requires it, but because Bulgaria is the most extreme available case of the condition. Nine million visitors a year, and the overwhelming majority leave having seen the three things everyone sees, in a country with layers underneath them that almost nobody has been told about. If it works here, it works anywhere with a memory and a road.

---

## III. WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY MAKING

We are not in the information business. Information has been free for twenty-five years and it has moved almost nobody anywhere.

What we make happens about ninety seconds after arrival. The wind is wrong. Your shoes are wrong. You are looking at what appears to be an ordinary field, or an ordinary rock face, and someone tells you what happened on this exact ground — and you look up.

That is the product. Not knowledge.

**The instant knowledge lands in a body that is standing in the right place.**

Ten years later, the way we delivered it will not appear in the memory at all. The road will. The weather will. Who was with you will. That is not a failure of our craft. That is the proof it worked.

---

## IV. THE TRANSFORMATION

Three states. This is the whole of what we do to a person.

**Visitor.** Arrives to look at a thing. The place is an object and they are outside it. Most travel begins and ends here.

**Witness.** The relationship reverses. The place stops being something you are looking at and becomes something that was here before you and will be here after, and you feel — briefly, physically — observed by it. Almost everyone has had this once. Almost nobody has had it on purpose.

**Custodian.** Now you carry it. Not as a fact. As a small obligation. You know something about a piece of ground that most people who live beside it do not know, and knowing it makes you slightly responsible for it continuing to be known.

Three rules about this arc:

It is not a rank and it is not earned by volume. One place, one afternoon, one honest reversal is a complete transformation. Someone who goes once and is changed has received everything we have to give.

It cannot be transmitted. Not by us, not by a better sentence, not by a better film. It only happens in the presence of the thing.

And it cannot be bought. Whatever anyone pays us, they are paying for the door. The walking through is theirs.

---

## V. WHY IT HAS TO BE PHYSICAL

The obvious objection: if the goal is wonder, why not simply make something wonderful? A film. A room. A headset. Deliver the awe without the four-hour drive.

Because the drive is not the cost of the awe. The drive **is** the mechanism.

A film happens to you. A place requires you to have decided, arranged, travelled, arrived, and been mildly uncomfortable. Every one of those steps is an act of consent, and by the time you are standing there you have already invested enough of yourself that the thing in front of you cannot be dismissed. That is why the same fact read on a sofa evaporates in a week and heard on site survives for a decade.

**Effort is not friction to be engineered away. Effort is what converts information into memory.**

This produces our most important structural rule, and it applies to everything we will ever build — every screen, page, book, exhibit, and technology not yet invented:

> **We are always the trailer. We are never the destination.**

Any product of ours that becomes somewhere a person wants to stay has failed at the only thing we do, no matter how good it is. If a technology arrives that can replace the visit, our job is not to adopt it. Our job is to use it to make people want the visit more.

We are one of the few things ever built that measures success by how quickly people put it down.

## VI. WHY WE GO TOGETHER

A discovery held alone decays. You know this. You have had experiences on your own that you can no longer quite prove happened, because there is nobody to say *do you remember*.

A memory with a witness is load-bearing. A memory without one is a claim.

So the unit we design for is not a person. It is a car with three or four people in it, arguing about where to go next. It is a parent whose child found the marker first. It is someone who moved away at twelve, standing next to a partner who has never been here, trying to explain what this ground is.

This is also, unromantically, how places survive. Not through archives. Through one person telling another with enough conviction that the second person gets in the car. Every shared discovery produces journeys we will never see, caused by travellers we never reached.

**Second-hand journeys are not a marketing side effect. They are the point.** The strongest thing we can build is not an experience — it is a person who cannot stop talking about a place.

---

## VII. THE LOOP WE PROTECT ABOVE ALL

Everything exists to keep this turning:

> *"I never knew this existed."*
> ↓
> *"I want to go."*
> ↓
> *"I'm going this weekend."*
> ↓
> *"I want another one."*

Every decision gets tested against the loop, and against nothing else. Not engagement. Not session length. Not anything a dashboard can show you.

**Anything that increases time with us and decreases time on the road has failed, regardless of what its numbers say.**

The most dangerous ideas we will ever have are the ones that work.

---

## VIII. WHAT WE OWE THE PLACES

We are in the business of sending strangers to quiet places. Success and damage arrive by the same road, in the same cars.

So:

**We will not send people where a place cannot receive them.** Fragility outranks demand. A site that would be diminished by arrivals does not go on any map of ours, at any price, and some things we know will stay unmarked permanently. Choosing not to send anyone is a legitimate outcome of our work.

**Volume is never the point.** Ten people who arrive understanding what they are standing in are worth more than a thousand who came for a photograph. If we ever find ourselves optimising for the second number, we have become the thing that ruins places.

**The people who live there are not scenery.** They are the reason the place still exists. Their consent matters, their version of the story outranks ours, and the value we generate should be visible to them before it is visible to us.

**Leave every place more able to receive the next arrival than it was before we sent the first.** That is the whole obligation. If we cannot meet it somewhere, we do not go there.

---

## IX. WHAT WE REFUSE TO BECOME

**Not an encyclopedia.** Facts that do not move feet are not our business.

**Not a scoreboard.** No points, no streaks, no rankings, no manufactured anxiety about returning to a screen. The moment what someone carries away becomes a number, it stops being a memory.

**Not a brochure.** We will never write *charming*, *picturesque*, *hidden gem*, or *must-see*. Places that need adjectives are places we have failed to explain. A cliff that has been watched for two thousand six hundred years needs context and a person standing on it, not a synonym for beautiful.

**Not decorated mysticism.** We work with real graves, real faiths, living communities, and unresolved history. Nothing here is dressed up. Where we interpret, it will be obvious that we are interpreting. We will not invent a past for anyone.

**Not a technology company.** Every tool we use is optional and none of it is ever the reason. The day we design a place around a capability instead of a capability around a place, stop and reread this page.

**Never a product that keeps you.** Not by subscription, not by streak, not by habit, not by any mechanism that makes leaving feel like loss.

---

## X. HOW WE KNOW WE SUCCEEDED

Not downloads. Not ratings. Not minutes.

Cars on a road on a Saturday morning in April that were not there last April.

A village of two hundred people whose one coffee stand sells out by noon, and whose children can see a reason to stay.

Someone born in this country, standing in a place inside its borders, saying out loud: *I did not know this was here.*

A woman in Berlin who has now come four times, is missing eleven places, and has already booked the fifth trip.

A seventy-year-old who finished, because the words were big enough to read in direct sun and nothing ever required them to hold up a camera.

A family still arguing in the car about which one to do next.

Someone who never used anything we made, standing somewhere extraordinary because a friend would not stop talking about it.

And the truest measure, the one no analytics tool will ever report:

**Somewhere, a place that was going to be forgotten is not going to be forgotten, because enough strangers kept showing up — and showed up well.**

---

## THE SENTENCE

Every person who works on this — writer, engineer, translator, designer, historian, partner, guide — carries one sentence:

> # If it does not end with someone standing somewhere they had to go to be, we have failed.

*There* is the real place, whenever a person can reach it. When they cannot — because of age, money, distance, or a body that will not allow it — *there* is the nearest real thing they can stand in front of, in a room, in a school, in their own town. The obligation does not change. It has to end in the physical world, in a body, somewhere they had to go.

Everything else is detail.
